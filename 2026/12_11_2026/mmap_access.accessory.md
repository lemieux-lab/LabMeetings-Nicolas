# Figure: mmap access and why a full session is not penalised

What it shows: top left, what is in RAM after load (sorted k-mer index and block_ptr, 265 GB RSS, the ~45 min load). Top right, the three steps of a peptide query: sorted tiles, findfirst in RAM (~36 µs, never touches the blob), then block number and offset from block_ptr. Middle, the 2.1 TiB counts blob as a row of 64-k-mer blocks, memory-mapped. Grey cells are never read. Orange cells are cold first touches, green cells are warm neighbors served from the same page-cache read. Bottom, two boxes contrasting random access (every row a first touch, ~17 ms, the B2 1e6-row sweep ~387x slower) with a sorted peptide query (few first touches, rest warm ~61 µs, 1,245 peptides in 0.5 s).

Real numbers: 265 GB RSS, 2.1 TiB blob, 64 k-mers per block, ~36 µs findfirst, ~17 ms cold, ~61 µs warm, 387x, 0.5 s. Derived, not measured: ~2.4 KB per block (64 x 37.76 B/k-mer) and the "few hundred neighboring blocks per 1 MB read" claim (1 MB NFS rsize / 2.4 KB). Illustrative: which cells are colored, cell counts.

Caveat on the claim: that a peptide's k-mers share blocks is an inference from sorted order, it has not been measured directly (no counter of distinct blocks touched per session).

Style: dark card, green warm/in-RAM, orange cold, gold for the query, gray neutral. Connector coordinates are absolute, no transforms.

Revision (2026-11-11): most cells now green (in page cache), a minority grey (not cached yet), 3 orange. Reason: oni has 1.8 TiB RAM and the index takes ~265 GB, leaving roughly 1.5 TiB of page cache against a 2.1 TiB blob, so around 70% of it can stay resident in principle. That is an upper bound shared with other jobs on the node, and blocks are only cached after first read. The dashed connector now runs from the step 3 box, left along the gap between panels, and down to the first orange cell (absolute coordinates, no transforms). Panel title moved right so the connector does not cross it.

Revision 2 (2026-11-11, reworked after feedback to show how a cold touch also loads the following parts): the figure is now a three-step timeline over one row of blob blocks. Step 1, nothing cached, the query wants block 14 (gold outline, reached by a dashed arrow from the query strip: findfirst in RAM, block (i-1)÷64, offset from block_ptr). Step 2, block 14 is cold (orange, ~17 ms) and a single ~1 MB NFS read brings in block 14 plus the blocks that follow (green, bracketed). Step 3, the next sorted lookups (blocks 15, 16, 19, 22) hit the already-cached window at ~61 µs, and a lookup outside the window (block 33) triggers a new cold read with its own window. Bottom boxes contrast random access with a sorted peptide query. The RAM panel from revision 1 was folded into the top strip. The previous single-panel version is kept as `mmap_access_v2_single_panel.svg`.

Derived, not measured: ~2.4 KB per block (64 x 37.76 B), so a 1 MB read covers a few hundred blocks. Window width (12 cells) and which blocks are asked for are illustrative. Assumption worth knowing: that the read brings in the blocks that follow, and not also those before, is a simplification of kernel readahead behavior.
